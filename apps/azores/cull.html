Cull

Everything up to the last step happens in this browser: no photo leaves the machine until you press upload. The mechanical cuts — wrong dates, bursts, near-duplicates, soft frames — are made here for free. The one judgement a machine is actually needed for, is this a good picture of us, gets a short list handed to a vision model, and then you get the last word. Save this page to disk and it works the same way with the radio off; only the last step goes near a network.

1 · Point it at the camera roll

Drop the whole export folder in. Nothing is uploaded and nothing is modified — the page reads each file, measures it, and forgets it when you close the tab.

Drag a folder here, or

JPEG, PNG, WebP and MP4/MOV read everywhere. HEIC only decodes in Safari on a Mac — in Chrome those files are counted and skipped, so either run this page in Safari or convert first: sips -s format jpeg *.HEIC --out jpg/

2 · Trip window

Prefilled from the itinerary. Capture time comes from EXIF where the file has it, the MP4/MOV header for video, and the file's own date only as a last resort — copying a camera roll around often rewrites that, so anything falling back is flagged rather than trusted.

3 · Collapse the bursts and duplicates

Frames are grouped when they were taken close together and look alike (a 64-bit perceptual hash, so a re-crop or a slight pan still matches). Within each group the sharpest, best-exposed frame wins. Sharpness is the variance of a Laplacian — the honest version of "is it in focus" — and clipping is the share of pixels crushed to pure black or blown to pure white.

4 · Hand the short list to a vision model

Twenty-five frames ride on one sheet, so a 250-frame short list becomes ten images rather than 250. That is the whole reason this step is cheap enough to actually run — and it is the only step that wants a model at all. Save the sheets next to the photos, hand the folder to Claude with the prompt below, and bring back a verdict.json.

5 · Your review — nothing is loaded until you say so

Click a tile to keep or cut it. Click the duplicate badge to see everything that was collapsed behind it, in case the machine kept the wrong one. The day counters go red when a day of the trip has nothing in it.

6 · Load them into the gallery

Each photo is resized to 2000px in the browser before it goes, which also strips EXIF — including the GPS tag, which should not be published on an open page. Uploads run newest-day-first so the gallery, which shows newest first, reads day one at the top. Captions are prefilled from the itinerary; the model's line is used where you accepted one. This is the first and only step that needs a connection — everything before it ran on your own machine.
